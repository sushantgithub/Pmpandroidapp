# PMP study notes (original)

Independent exam commentary. **Not PMI courseware.** Not a copy, extract, or substitute for the *PMBOK® Guide*, *The Standard for Project Management*, or the Exam Content Outline. PMI, PMP, and PMBOK are PMI marks. Read the official publications for the source material; use this file to **practice how to think** on test day.

How this was written: the Eighth Edition table of contents was used only as a **topic checklist**. Every paragraph below is original wording for study and recall.

---

## How to use these notes

1. Skim a section, then cover it and recap out loud in 60 seconds.
2. For every situational item, run **A.R.T.**: Assess → Review artifacts/agreements → Take the smallest professional action.
3. Name the **approach** first (predictive / adaptive / hybrid) before you pick a tool.
4. Drill formulas until SPI, CPI, and EAC are automatic.
5. Sleep and full-length mocks matter more than rereading.

Exam snapshot (public ECO facts): **180** items, **230** minutes, two optional **10-minute** breaks, mix of item types. Domains: **People ~42%**, **Process ~50%**, **Business Environment ~8%**. Roughly half the scored work feels predictive and half agile/hybrid. PMI does not publish a raw cut score.

## 1. What a project is (and is not)

A **project** is temporary and unique: it creates a product, service, or result, then ends. **Operations** keep the lights on. Mixing them in your head causes bad answers (treating BAU as a project, or never closing a project).

**Governance** is how the organization decides, funds, and oversees work. Project governance should line up with that, not fight it.

**Portfolio** aligns work to strategy. **Program** coordinates related projects for extra benefits. **Project** delivers a defined result. If the stem is about strategy mix, think portfolio. If it is about shared benefits across projects, think program.

---

## 2. Value delivery, environment, and roles

**Value** is why the work exists: benefits, not busy-ness. Success is more than “on time / on budget.” Ask who receives the benefit and when.

**EEFs** are conditions you inherit (market, culture, regulations, infrastructure). **OPAs** are what the organization already owns (templates, lessons, procedures). You tailor inside those constraints; you do not pretend they are optional.

**Organizational structure** sets PM power:

| Shape | Typical PM authority |
| --- | --- |
| Functional / organic | Low |
| Weak matrix | Low |
| Balanced matrix | Shared — negotiate |
| Strong matrix | Moderate to high |
| Project-oriented | High |

**PMO** flavors: supportive (templates), controlling (compliance), directive (runs projects).

Roles to keep straight:

- **Sponsor / customer / product owner** — funds, value, or backlog order (title depends on approach).
- **Project management team** — integrate, coordinate, remove blockers.
- **Team** — does the work; in agile they self-manage *how*.
- **End users and other stakeholders** — must be identified more than once.

Functions you will see in stems: oversight, feedback, facilitation, doing the work, expertise, organizational direction, providing resources. The exam often asks *who should act*, not *who has the fanciest title*.

## 3. Six principles (study labels)

Treat these as **habits**, not slogans to quote.

1. **Holistic view** — every decision has side effects on people, risk, cost, and later operations.
2. **Focus on value** — drop or reshape work that does not serve the benefit case.
3. **Embed quality** — build it in; do not inspect heroics at the end.
4. **Accountable leadership** — integrity, trust, psychological safety; leadership can be shared.
5. **Sustainability** — environmental, social, and economic effects are part of the design, not a poster at closeout.
6. **Empowered culture** — push decisions to the people closest to the work, within agreed constraints.

Mindset dimensions that show up in good answers: **proactive**, **ownership**, **value-driven**. Bad answers skip analysis, hide bad news, or dump unsolved problems on the sponsor.

## 4. Life cycles, approaches, cadence, focus areas

**Phase** = chunk of work with exit criteria. **Gate** = go / no-go / change before the next chunk. Process groups (focus areas) are **not** the same as phases; you can plan and control inside every phase.

**Predictive** — requirements stable enough to baseline; change control is formal.  
**Adaptive** — uncertainty is high; learn by delivering slices.  
**Hybrid** — lock what must be locked (law, contract date); keep empirical the uncertain middle.

Choose based on **deliverable uncertainty**, **project risk**, and **organization culture** — not fashion.

**Cadence**: one delivery, many deliveries, or a regular heartbeat. Cadence is about *when value shows up*, not whether you planned.

**Five focus areas** (classic process groups with a practical name):

| Focus area | You are mostly… |
| --- | --- |
| Initiating | Authorizing, naming stakeholders, setting purpose |
| Planning | Elaborating how value will be created and controlled |
| Executing | Coordinating people and work |
| Monitoring and controlling | Measuring, comparing, correcting, routing change |
| Closing | Accepting, transferring, archiving, releasing |

Verbs that leak the focus area: plan/estimate/define (planning); manage/conduct/implement (executing); monitor/control/validate (M&C); finalize/close/transition (closing).

## 5. Performance domains (seven)

These absorb older “knowledge area” labels. You still do integration, quality, communications, and procurement — the **map** moved.

### Governance

Glue of the project: purpose, decision rights, integration, much of **quality**, and change control. Charter authorizes; the sponsor issues it (you may help write it). The plan integrates subsidiary plans and **baselines**. After baselines exist, **every** baseline change needs impact analysis and the agreed path (often a CCB). Rank does not skip process.

Close in order: **formal acceptance → knowledge transfer / lessons → transition → release people**. Do not disband first.

### Scope

Requirements have an owner and a test. Trace them. Predictive baseline = scope statement + WBS + WBS dictionary. **Validate** = acceptance with the customer. **Control** = stop silent growth. Gold plating is still extra scope. Adaptive: product owner orders the backlog; Definition of Done is the quality contract.

### Schedule

Define → sequence → estimate → develop → control. Critical path is the **longest** path (usually zero total float). **Crash** = more resources (cost). **Fast-track** = overlap (risk). Neither is step one if you have not analyzed or talked about tradeoffs.

Estimating: analogous (fast, coarse), parametric (rate × quantity), three-point / PERT, bottom-up (slow, usually most accurate).

### Finance

Broader than “cost tracking”: funding, reserves, benefits, forecasts. **Contingency** (known-unknowns) sits with the project inside the baseline. **Management reserve** (unknown-unknowns) sits with management until released.

EVM anchors: **EV** (earned), **PV** (planned), **AC** (spent).

- SV = EV − PV  CV = EV − AC  
- SPI = EV / PV  CPI = EV / AC  
- Typical EAC = BAC / CPI  
- Remain-at-plan EAC = AC + (BAC − EV)  
- ETC = EAC − AC  VAC = BAC − EAC  
- TCPI to BAC = (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC)

Index **&lt; 1** or negative variance is unfavorable. Early estimates are wide; later estimates should be tighter.

### Stakeholders

Identify **again** when new people appear. Map power/interest. Move people toward the engagement you need (unaware → resistant → neutral → supportive → leading). Communications live here: push / pull / interactive. Sensitive conflict: live conversation. Channels = **n(n − 1)/2**.

### Resources

People, equipment, materials, and much of **procurement**. RACI: **one Accountable** per activity. Tuckman storming is normal — facilitate it. Servant leadership: remove impediments this week. Make-or-buy, SOW, fair seller process, contract type matches how clear the scope is (FFP when clear; cost-plus when not; T&M with a cap). Ethics: do not shop one bidder’s price to another.

### Risk

Risk = future and uncertain. Issue = already happening. Different logs. Flow: identify continuously → qualitative (and quantitative when it pays) → respond → implement → watch. Threats: escalate, avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept. Opportunities: escalate, exploit, share, enhance, accept. **Residual** remains after a response; **secondary** is born from the response. EMV = probability × money.

## 6. Tailoring

Tailoring is **deliberate**, not “we skipped docs because we are agile.”

1. Pick a starting approach (predictive / adaptive / hybrid).  
2. Adjust for the **organization** (governance, PMO, culture).  
3. Adjust for **this project** (risk, team skill, constraints).  
4. **Inspect** (retrospectives, issues, quality, stakeholder feedback) and adjust again.

Tailor life cycle, processes, and how you engage people. If quality is falling or the team is drowning in ceremony, that is a tailoring signal.

## 7. Inputs, outputs, tools (how to study them)

Do **not** memorize every ITTO table. For each domain, remember:

- A **trigger** (why you would start this work)
- A **few typical inputs** (charter, plan, logs, EEFs/OPAs)
- A **few typical outputs** (updated plan, change request, accepted deliverable)
- **Tools** that match uncertainty: expert judgment and meetings everywhere; data analysis when numbers matter; facilitation when people disagree

If two tools look right, pick the one that **fits the approach in the stem**.

## 8. PMO, AI, procurement extras

**PMO** earns its keep by making it easier to deliver value — not by becoming a paperwork factory. Customer-centric PMOs serve project teams and sponsors.

**AI** is a tool: data quality, privacy, bias, and **human accountability** still belong to the project. Useful for drafting, clustering risks, or status — not for secretly changing baselines or faking stakeholder agreement.

**Procurement** extras: claims need facts and ethics, not ego. Legal fights are last, not first. Contract type is a risk allocation choice.

## 9. Agile / Scrum (still half the exam)

Agile is an **umbrella**, not one method. Manifesto: people, working product, customer collaboration, responding to change — *over* (not instead of) process, heavy docs, contract fights, and a frozen plan.

**Kanban**: visualize, limit WIP, manage flow. Cycle time = start to done. Throughput = items per time.

**Scrum**: transparency, inspection, adaptation. 3 roles, 5 events, 3 artifacts. Product owner orders the backlog. Scrum master coaches and unblocks. Developers own how to get to Done. Sprint is a timebox with a Done increment.

**People domain**: coach, conflict (collaborate first), ground rules the team helped write, virtual working agreements, no public humiliation.

**Process domain**: keep artifacts honest; hybrid honors both the CCB *and* the backlog.

**Business environment**: if the business case dies, recommend pause/pivot/cancel. New law = analyze compliance, then change/risk process. Safety = stop unsafe work first.

## 10. Formula sheet

| Name | Expression |
| --- | --- |
| EV | % complete × BAC (for that work) |
| SV / CV | EV − PV  EV − AC |
| SPI / CPI | EV / PV  EV / AC |
| EAC typical | BAC / CPI |
| EAC remain at plan | AC + (BAC − EV) |
| ETC / VAC | EAC − AC  BAC − EAC |
| TCPI to BAC | (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC) |
| PERT expected | (O + 4M + P) / 6 |
| Triangular | (O + M + P) / 3 |
| PERT σ | (P − O) / 6 |
| Channels | n(n − 1) / 2 |
| EMV | probability × impact |
| PTA (FPIF) | ((ceiling − target price) / buyer share) + target cost |
| Total float | LS − ES or LF − EF |

## 11. Exam traps (original)

- **Do first** ≠ crash/fast-track/cut scope. Analyze and align people first unless safety/law.
- **Might / could** = risk register. **Has happened** = issue log.
- **Always / never** are usually wrong except safety, ethics, and “all baseline changes go through change control.”
- Face-to-face (or live video) beats a spicy email for conflict.
- Do not fire, cancel a contract, or skip the CCB as a first move.
- Closure: acceptance before lessons and before releasing the team.
- Velocity is a **forecast aid**, not a weapon to compare teams.

## 12. Mini drills (original)

1. EV 80, PV 100, AC 90 — behind and over. (SV −20, CV −10)  
2. BAC 200, CPI 0.8 — typical EAC = 250.  
3. Team grows 6 → 8 — channels 15 → 28.  
4. “Might run out of testers next month” — risk, not issue.  
5. VP wants a “tiny” baseline add tonight — change request + impact, not heroics.  
6. Hybrid: regulation date + unknown UI — plan the legal wrapper; iterate the UI.  
7. Product backlog order — product owner.  
8. Business case dead — talk benefits with the sponsor; consider cancel.
